## Problem Statement

Turkish freelancers doing B2B corporate work get paid on 60-90 day terms. The money
arrives; the timing wrecks cash flow. The founder has this problem himself.

This is a **timing** problem, not a trust problem. That distinction drives every
decision below — non-payment, invoicing burden, and inability to contract without a
legal entity are three different products, and only one of them is being built.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `teklif` | Quote / proposal document sent to a client before work starts |
| `SMM` / `e-SMM` | *Serbest meslek makbuzu* — the receipt a self-employed professional issues instead of a company invoice; e-SMM is the electronic form regulated by the Revenue Administration |
| `stopaj` | Income tax withheld at source by the paying company (20% for self-employed professional income) |
| `temlik` | Assignment of a receivable to a third party — the legal step factoring depends on |
| Law 6493 | Turkish payment services law; holding third-party funds requires a licence under it |
| BDDK | Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency — licenses factoring and financing activity |
| Sub-merchant onboarding | PSP process for registering each payee under a marketplace account so the PSP can split and pay out |
| Concierge test | Delivering the service by hand — real contracts, manual bank transfers, a spreadsheet ledger — to learn whether anyone wants it, before writing the software that would automate it |

## Demand Evidence

Weak, and named as such. Founder-as-user only:

- No one has paid. No one has committed to pay. No user interviews. No waitlist.
- The founder is a real user with real recurring pain, which beats an imagined persona
  but is a market of one.
- **The riskiest assumption is untested:** asked whether a client would prefund an
  escrow milestone before work starts, the answer was *"I haven't asked anyone this
  directly."* Every table in the schema depends on that behaviour.

## Status Quo

Freelancer invoices via SMM, 20% stopaj withheld at source by the paying company. Then
waits 60-90 days. Current workaround for the timing gap: absorb it.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Segment is the open strategic question, not the primitive.** Net-90 is an enterprise
procurement artifact. Auto-release plus prefund-at-signature is plausible against
20-person agencies, startups, and foreign clients — who already do 50% upfront routinely
— and implausible against enterprise procurement.

**Wedge:** a Turkish contract/teklif with the stopaj/SMM math shown, an auto-release
clause, and a payment link. Not a platform.

## Constraints

- Money as integer kuruş; fee and tax identities must hold exactly. The authoritative
  statement is in `CLAUDE.md`; in short, `clientCharge - fee == gross` and
  `withholding + net == gross`, both exact, with every derived figure computed by
  subtraction rather than a second percentage. (Revised 2026-08-30: the fee is charged to
  the client on top of the gross rather than withheld from it — see
  `pricing-client-pays-model.md`.)
- No Docker on the dev machine — schema changes verify against hosted Supabase only.
- Holding third-party funds is licensed under Law 6493. Financing is separately licensed
  under the BDDK factoring regime.

## Premises

The founder rejected all four of the session's opening premises. The independent second
opinion then vindicated three of them, and the premises were revised:

- **REVISED — escrow can fix timing.** The original premise ("custody doesn't address
  timing") missed *auto-release on inaction*. With a clock on SUBMITTED→COMPLETED, the
  default flips from "pay when someone gets around to it" to "pay unless you object
  within N days." That converts an unbounded delay into a bounded one, and is the one
  thing a freelancer cannot do alone. **This is now the load-bearing mechanism and must
  be validated directly — see The Assignment, question 2.**
- **REVISED — prefunding is a segment question, not a refusal.** Prefunding negotiated
  at contract signature (when terms are negotiable) is not the same ask as prefunding at
  invoice time. 50% upfront is routine for agencies and startups.
- **HELD — custody is solvable but is not a moat.** remotify.co already ships the
  reseller/intermediary structure that sidesteps the licensing wall. Feasibility is
  better than feared; differentiation cannot be "we hold funds safely."
- **HELD — the existing schema is an asset, not decoration.** The state machine and
  audit ledger are a verified record of what was delivered and accepted, and when — the
  underwriting data no Turkish factoring company has for individuals. Relevant only if
  the financing branch reopens.

**Deferred: financing.** The session considered and withdrew financing as the primary
primitive. At 20-80k TRY tickets a 3% discount over 60 days yields 600-2,400 TRY per
invoice, against manual underwriting of someone with no credit file, weak `temlik` on an
SMM, and a required BDDK licence plus balance sheet. The 48 Turkish factoring companies
skipping this segment is evidence against financing, not against escrow. One cheap
question in The Assignment closes this branch for good.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A1: Interviews + manual contract (CHOSEN — this week)
Effort S · Risk Low. No product. Ten structured conversations with client-side finance
people, plus a hand-written Turkish contract PDF used on the founder's own next gig.
Money by bank transfer, spreadsheet as ledger. Requires no brand, no domain, no code.

### Approach A2: Contract/teklif generator + PSP payment link (deferred to stage 2)
Effort M · Risk Med. The one-page generator: client, amount, milestones → shareable
agreement with stopaj/SMM math, auto-release clause, and a PSP payment link. Deferred
because the auto-release clause is legal drafting, not a form field, and because it is
wasted work if A1 finds no workable approval path. Needs a lawyer to review the clause.

### Approach B: Auto-release escrow on a licensed PSP, SMB-first
Effort L · Risk Med. Build the real mechanism riding a licensed PSP's split-payment
product to sidestep 6493 custody. Rejected for now: weeks of build before learning
whether any client will prefund, and sub-merchant onboarding is slow.

### Approach C: Workflow now, financing later off proprietary delivery data
Effort XL · Risk High. Rejected for now: needs a BDDK licence and a balance sheet, and it
is the longest path to testing whether the core premise is even true.

## Recommended Approach

**A1 now, A2 only if A1 passes.** The riskiest assumption is client prefunding
behaviour, and A1 tests it this week with zero code. Nothing is wasted: the contract
terms and stopaj math carry into A2, B and C unchanged. B remains the destination once a
real client funds a real milestone.

## Open Questions

- **Which client segment?** Resolved by the per-segment thresholds in Success Criteria —
  agency and enterprise are scored separately and imply different companies.
- **What auto-release window is acceptable?** Answered by Question 2 — anchor at five
  business days and record the window each interviewee actually names.
- **Does the reseller/intermediary structure hold under Turkish law**, or does it require

## Success Criteria

### What counts as a "workable approval path"

The load-bearing judgment call, so it is defined rather than left to taste. An answer
counts **only if the interviewee names both**:

1. the specific instrument — purchase order, advance against proforma, prepayment
   approval, vendor deposit; and
2. the person or step that approves it.

"I'd have to check with our CFO," "in principle yes, we've never done it," and "probably
fine" do **not** count. "PO raised by the project owner, approved by finance if under
50k, vendor onboarding takes two weeks" does.

### Stage 1 — decision date 2026-09-18

Ten conversations, segmented **6 from 10-50 person agencies/startups, 4 from
enterprise**. Each segment is scored **independently**, because a yes from each implies a
different company.

**Step 1 — score prefunding per segment:**

| Segment | Passes at | If it passes |
|---|---|---|
| Agency/startup | ≥3 of 6 | Agency wedge live. This is the expected path. |
| Enterprise | ≥2 of 4 | Enterprise viable — larger, slower, longer sales cycle. |

Either segment passing on its own is a **pass**, not a partial failure. Agency passing
while enterprise scores zero is the *expected good outcome*: build for agencies, stop
calling enterprise. Enterprise passing while agency fails is also a real result — it just
points at a slower, more sales-led company than the one currently designed.

**Both segments below threshold → KILL.** Prefunded escrow is dead; stop.

**Partial completion.** A segment needs **≥4 agency or ≥3 enterprise calls completed** to
be scored at all. Below that it is UNSCORED — extend that segment by two weeks rather
than reading a verdict into three conversations. A passing score on the other segment
still counts; do not hold a live result hostage to a slow one.

**Step 2 — score auto-release across all completed calls:**
**Passes at ≥5 of 10 naming a window of ≤15 business days as signable.** If the median
window named exceeds the 60-90 day delay it exists to replace, the mechanism is dead.

**Step 3 — combine. This is the actual go/no-go:**

| Prefunding | Auto-release | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Pass | Pass | **Proceed to A2 as designed.** Both halves of the thesis hold. |
| Pass | **Fail** | **Proceed to A2 *without* auto-release.** Clients will prefund but won't pre-commit to automatic release. What remains is prefunded milestones with a contractual approval deadline — still a genuine cash-flow win (paid at delivery, not delivery+90), but the product is a contract-and-payment tool, not escrow. Do **not** build Approach B. Re-scope first. |
| Fail | Pass | **Dead.** They like the release mechanism but won't fund it. There is no money in the account to release automatically. |
| Fail | Fail | **KILL.** |

The Pass/Fail row is the most likely real outcome. It does not kill the company; it
shrinks it into something smaller and faster to ship, and it means the Supabase state
machine stays on the shelf.

**Financing branch:** <4 of 10 freelancers accept the discount → branch closed
permanently. The test offers 4% over 75 days rather than the 3%/60 used in the economics
above, deliberately: if freelancers refuse even the *more generous* spread, the tighter
one is certainly dead.

**CONFIRM** = a segment passes **and** one real milestone is funded. Because a gig may
not kick off in time, CONFIRM resolves at **gig-start + 7 days, independent of the
decision date**. If no gig starts before 2026-09-18, a segment pass alone is sufficient
to proceed — do not stall on a gig that may not arrive.

**"Milestone funded" means** cleared funds in the account, ≥50% of the first milestone,
within 7 calendar days of the client receiving the agreement. Touch count (written
follow-ups, calls) is **logged as data, not a gate** — concierge work is high-touch by
design, and the count is what makes a later automated result comparable.

### Stage 2 — only if Stage 1 passes (A2)

Twenty external clients. **≥3 of 20 funding a first milestone within 7 days = proceed to
Approach B; fewer than 3 = prefunded escrow is dead even with a validated approval path.**
Scope and duration to be set when Stage 1 resolves.

## The Assignment

Three questions, same ten calls. Outreach starts **Monday 2026-08-31**. Record every
answer in `docs/research/prefunding-interviews.md` — one row per person: segment, company
size, answer, **the approval path in their own words**, the auto-release window they name,
and **the freelancer count and annual spend** from question 3.
Freelancer discount answers go in a second table in the same file: name, accept/refuse.

**Question 1 — prefunding:**
> "Would you fund 50% of the first milestone of a freelance engagement at kickoff, into a
> third-party account, at a 10% service fee on top of the contract amount — and what's the
> approval path for that internally?"

(The question deliberately describes the A2/B end state. In A1 there is no third party —
funds land in a separate account the founder controls. Ask about the real product, not
the concierge stand-in; the approval path you are collecting is the one that has to exist
later.)

**The fee is part of the question, not a detail to omit.** Under the pricing decision of
2026-08-30 the service fee is charged to the client on top of the contract amount, not
withheld from the freelancer — so the buyer is being asked for two things at once: pay
earlier, and pay 10% more. A "yes" collected without the fee validates a product that is
not being sold. If an interviewee balks specifically at the fee rather than at prefunding,
record that separately: it is a pricing result, not a thesis result, and the two must not
be scored together. See `pricing-client-pays-model.md`.

**Question 2 — auto-release (do not skip this one):**
> "If the freelancer delivered and you hadn't objected within a fixed window — say five
> business days — would automatic release of that payment be approvable? And what's the
> longest window you'd be willing to sign?"

Anchor at five, then record the number *they* give. Question 2 tests the mechanism the
entire revised premise now rests on; Question 1 alone would validate a product you are no
longer building.

**Question 3 — the expansion multiplier:**
> "How many different freelancers does your company work with in a year, and roughly how
> much do you pay them in total?"

Cheap to ask, and it decides the growth architecture. The acquisition unit today is a
freelancer who already has a client, which means the platform never has to generate
demand — but it also means nothing compounds. The way out is the *client account*: the
freelancer brings one company in, and that company already works with N other freelancers.
This question measures N.

Not a kill gate. A low N does not invalidate prefunding or auto-release; it only says the
client account is not a flywheel and growth has to come from somewhere else. Score it
separately: **N ≥ 10 across the median respondent means the client account is the
expansion unit and no marketplace is needed. N ≤ 3 means expansion has to be re-thought**
— see the marketplace decision in `pricing-client-pays-model.md`.

Ask ten *clients*, not ten freelancers. Freelancers already want this. The buyer is the
constraint, and the buyer has never been asked. The approval path is the deliverable, not
the yes or no.

**Separately, five minutes:** ask ten freelancers "96% today or 100% in 75 days?" If
fewer than 4 accept, the financing branch closes permanently.
